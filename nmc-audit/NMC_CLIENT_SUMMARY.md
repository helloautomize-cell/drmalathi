# What the new NMC advertising rules mean for your website

**Trichy Fetal Medicine and Fertility Centre — plain-language summary**
Prepared 26 October 2026 · Covers fmctrichy.com and the website source code

## The short version

The National Medical Commission's new ethics guidelines (in force from October 2026) set strict rules for what a hospital or doctor may publish: no testimonials, no patient photos or outcome promises, no "best/first" claims unless independently provable, every doctor's medical council registration number must be shown, and no paid marketing can be publicly attributed.

Your website was built with many of these rules already in mind — no patient photos, no prices or offers, no pop-ups, honest PCPNDT notices, and a proper consent checkbox on every form. The audit found **3 high-priority items** and a handful of smaller gaps. Nothing needs to be rebuilt; most fixes are small text or data changes.

## What we will change

1. **Add registration numbers.** Each doctor's Tamil Nadu Medical Council registration number will be shown on their profile, the doctors page, and the site footer. *We need the certificate numbers from you.*

2. **Change the agency credit.** The footer line "Built & Marketed by Automize Media Labs" will be shortened to "Website: Automize Media Labs" — the rules do not allow a public credit for marketing a medical practice.

3. **Remove the patient-review feature.** Three patient quotes are saved in the system waiting for consent before going live. Under the new rules consent does not make testimonials allowed, so we will remove the feature and the saved quotes entirely instead of switching them on.

4. **Add honest risk lines to service pages.** Every scan/procedure page will carry a short, doctor-approved note on what the test cannot show and any risks — the design already has a place for this.

5. **Keep the "first in Tamil Nadu" claims off.** These claims are already hidden on the live site. We recommend removing them permanently unless you have independent documentary proof (a registry or official record). We will never switch them on without it.

6. **Label AI images and tidy disclosures.** The homepage family illustration was generated with AI — it will carry a small "AI-generated illustration" note, and the editorial policy will explain how AI is used. The privacy page will name a grievance contact and fix a self-contradicting retention line. The "Reviewed" badge on articles will appear only where a named doctor has actually reviewed the article.

7. **Clean up guide endings.** Our 60 patient guides currently end with a promotional line inviting readers to contact the centre. That sentence will be removed — the articles will stay purely educational, with the normal contact options below the article.

## What we need from you

- Medical council registration number for each doctor (Dr Malathi, Dr Revathy, Dr Nivethitha)
- PCPNDT registration number and Clinical Establishments Act registration number
- Confirmation of whether IUI/IVF is offered (a gallery caption mentions IVF theatres — if so, ART registration also applies)
- A name and contact for the DPDP grievance officer (can be an existing staff role)
- Confirmation of the founding year (the site says "since 2012" / "14 years") and weekly opening hours
- Any documentary evidence for the "first" claims, or agreement to drop them
- Which doctor has reviewed which patient guide, so the "Reviewed by" badge is accurate

## What stays

Your strong points need no change: plain-language guides, same-visit reporting, the scan-timing finder, honest PCPNDT compliance messaging, and a privacy policy that already meets the new data-protection law. None of the fixes reduce how patients find you — the changes keep the content that search and AI answer engines actually cite.
