Pregnancy After Recurrent Miscarriage: How It Is Monitored

Pregnant again after repeated miscarriages? Learn about early scans, medicines, tests and emotional support that make the next pregnancy safer and less stressful.

Quick answer

Pregnancy after recurrent miscarriage is monitored with early ultrasound scans (often at 6 to 7 weeks and again a week or two later), regular follow-up visits, treatment for any cause that was found, such as aspirin and heparin for antiphospholipid syndrome or progesterone in selected women, and emotional support. Most women who reach the stage of a heartbeat on scan go on to deliver a healthy baby.

A positive pregnancy test after repeated losses brings joy mixed with fear. Many women count every day. Supportive monitoring can give reassurance and, where needed, treatment that improves the chance of a healthy baby.

What Is the Outlook?

The outlook is better than most couples fear. Even after three or more miscarriages, the chance of a successful next pregnancy is about 60 to 75 percent, depending on age and cause. The chance is higher when a heartbeat is seen and in younger women. Close supervision and support are known to improve outcomes and reduce anxiety.

What Happens in the Early Weeks?

Confirm the pregnancy

A urine or blood test confirms pregnancy. If you are unsure about timing or have pain or bleeding, hCG blood levels may be repeated to check they are rising appropriately.

Early scan

  • 6 to 7 weeks: A transvaginal scan checks that the pregnancy is in the uterus and, if the heartbeat is visible, offers reassurance.
  • Repeat scan after 1 to 2 weeks: Confirms growth and heartbeat. Some clinics offer additional scans to ease anxiety.

An early scan cannot prevent a miscarriage, but it provides information and allows early planning.

Start treatment promptly

Depending on your tests:

  • Antiphospholipid syndrome: Low-dose aspirin and low molecular weight heparin injections.
  • Thyroid disease: Thyroxine and TSH monitoring.
  • Diabetes: Tight sugar control.
  • Progesterone: Considered in women with early bleeding and a history of one or more miscarriages. It may improve live birth in women with three or more losses and bleeding in early pregnancy. Your doctor will decide.
  • Folic acid and other supplements as advised.

Medicines without proven benefit, such as steroids, immunoglobulin or intralipid for unexplained loss, are not recommended routinely.

What Scans Come Later?

  • 11 to 13 weeks: NT scan and first-trimester screening.
  • NIPT or double marker if you wish.
  • 18 to 22 weeks: Detailed anomaly scan (TIFFA).
  • Cervical length scan: For women with second-trimester loss, a short cervix may be checked, sometimes with a stitch (cerclage) advised.
  • Growth scans and Doppler: In the third trimester if risks are present, such as antiphospholipid syndrome or high blood pressure.

What About Bed Rest?

Strict bed rest has not been shown to prevent miscarriage and increases the risk of blood clots. Gentle activity is usually safe unless advised otherwise. Follow your doctor's guidance if you have bleeding.

What Should You Do If You Have Bleeding or Pain?

Light spotting is common in early pregnancy and does not always signal miscarriage. Contact your doctor if you have:

  • Heavy bleeding or passing clots
  • Severe lower abdominal pain
  • Shoulder-tip pain, dizziness or fainting
  • Fever or foul discharge

Seek urgent care for heavy bleeding with pain.

How Can You Look After Your Mind?

  • Ask your doctor about a supportive care clinic, where available.
  • Share your feelings with a trusted person.
  • Avoid constant searching online.
  • Practise relaxation, short walks and enough sleep.
  • Remember that anxiety does not cause miscarriage.
  • Seek counselling if fear is overwhelming.

When Should You See a Doctor?

See a specialist as soon as the pregnancy test is positive, and book your first scan. Contact them at once for bleeding, pain or any concern.

Final Thoughts

You do not have to go through this pregnancy alone. A caring team, clear plan and a bit of hope can make a big difference. For supportive monitoring, consult Dr Malathi G. Prasad, a fetal medicine specialist at Trichy Fetal Medicine and Fertility Centre in Trichy.

Key takeaways

  1. The chance of a healthy pregnancy after recurrent miscarriage is good, often above 60 to 75 percent.
  2. Early scans and regular visits provide reassurance.
  3. Treatment depends on the cause, such as aspirin and heparin for antiphospholipid syndrome.
  4. Routine bed rest is not advised.
  5. Emotional support is part of good care.
