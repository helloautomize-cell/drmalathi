What Is Chorionicity in Twin Pregnancy and Why It Matters

Chorionicity tells doctors whether twins share a placenta. Learn how it is found, why the first trimester scan is best, and how it changes your care in Trichy.

Quick answer

Chorionicity tells doctors whether twins have their own placentas or share one. It is best confirmed on an ultrasound in the first trimester, ideally between 11 and 14 weeks. Twins who share a placenta need closer monitoring because they face higher risks. Knowing this early helps plan safe, well-timed care.

Chorionicity means the number of placentas in a twin pregnancy. Twins can have two separate placentas (dichorionic) or share one (monochorionic). This single detail decides how often you need scans and which complications doctors watch for, so it is the first thing a fetal medicine specialist checks.

What Does Chorionicity Mean?

The chorion is the outer membrane around a baby, and it helps form the placenta. Chorionicity simply describes how the placentas and membranes are arranged.

There are three common patterns:

  • Dichorionic diamniotic (DCDA): two placentas and two sacs
  • Monochorionic diamniotic (MCDA): one shared placenta and two sacs
  • Monochorionic monoamniotic (MCMA): one shared placenta and one sac, which is rare

How Is Chorionicity Different From Zygosity?

Zygosity is about whether twins came from one egg or two. Chorionicity is about the placenta. The two are linked but not the same.

  • Twins from two eggs (non-identical) are always dichorionic.
  • Twins from one egg (identical) can be dichorionic or monochorionic, depending on when the egg split.

So chorionicity cannot always tell you if twins are identical, but it tells doctors what matters for care.

How Do Doctors Find Out?

Doctors use ultrasound. In the first trimester, they look for a "lambda" or "twin peak" sign, a small triangle of tissue between the two sacs, which points to two placentas. A thin "T sign" points to a shared placenta.

This works best early. After about 14 weeks, the signs become harder to read, and mistakes are more likely. That is why an early scan with an experienced sonologist is so valuable.

Why Does Chorionicity Matter?

Dichorionic twins

Each baby has its own blood supply. Risks are similar to other twin pregnancies, such as preterm birth or differences in growth. Scans are usually advised every 4 weeks from the second trimester.

Monochorionic twins

The babies share one placenta, and blood vessels connect them. This can cause:

  • [Twin-to-twin](/guides/twin-pregnancy-complications-doctors-look-for) transfusion syndrome (TTTS), where blood flows unevenly between babies
  • Selective growth restriction, where one baby grows much less
  • Twin anemia polycythemia sequence (TAPS), a milder imbalance in blood levels

Because of these risks, scans are usually advised every 2 weeks from about 16 weeks.

What Does This Mean for Your Care in Trichy?

If you are expecting twins, try to have your first scan with a fetal medicine specialist between 11 and 14 weeks. Ask directly, "Is this DCDA or MCDA?" and keep the report safe. Every doctor who sees you later will rely on it.

Do not worry if your twins share a placenta. Most monochorionic twins do well with regular scans, and problems found early can often be treated.

Final Thoughts

A twin pregnancy brings double joy and a few extra questions. Chorionicity is the foundation of your care plan, and getting it right early makes every later scan more meaningful. To confirm it and plan your follow-up, you can consult Dr Malathi G. Prasad, a fetal medicine specialist at Trichy Fetal Medicine and Fertility Centre in Trichy.

Key takeaways

  1. Chorionicity means whether twins have one placenta or two.
  2. It is best identified on a scan between 11 and 14 weeks.
  3. Shared-placenta twins need scans about every 2 weeks.
  4. Separate-placenta twins are usually scanned every 4 weeks.
  5. Early, careful monitoring helps most twin pregnancies go well.
